**In short:** Poland is in the EU but does not use the euro. The currency is the złoty (PLN), and you will need it for anything you pay for in cash. If you are bringing euros, exchange them in Kraków, not at home or at the airport. Look at the **buy** rate next to EUR, compare it with the mid-market rate on your phone, and ask how many złoty you will receive before you hand over your notes. The euro is the currency we exchange most often. Visitors from Germany, Italy, Spain, France, Ireland, the Netherlands and the rest of the euro area arrive with it every day, and so do travellers from further away who picked up euros as their "European" cash. This guide collects the questions we hear most at the counter.

## Can you pay in euros in Kraków?

Expect to pay in złoty. Poland is a member of the European Union but has kept its own currency, so prices in shops, restaurants, museums and on public transport are in PLN. Some businesses on the tourist routes will take euro notes, but they set their own rate and usually give change in złoty, so it is rarely a good deal.

Cards and phones work almost everywhere. For those payments the only rule is to choose PLN when the terminal asks which currency to charge; the reason is explained in our guide to [paying in PLN and dynamic currency conversion](/knowledge-base/pay-in-pln-or-home-currency-dcc-krakow). Cash is still handy for market stalls, tips, small bakeries, public toilets and the occasional "cash only" bar. Our guide to [the cost of a weekend in Kraków](/knowledge-base/how-much-money-weekend-in-krakow) suggests how much to carry.

## Exchange at home or in Kraków?

Exchange offices earn their money on the gap between the rate at which they buy a currency and the rate at which they sell it. That gap, the spread, is narrow for currencies an office trades all day and wide for currencies it rarely sees.

In the euro area the złoty is a minor currency, so banks and bureaux there tend to quote a wide spread. In Kraków it is the other way round: euros arrive all day, offices compete for them, and you can compare several boards within a few minutes' walk. Airports are the exception in every country. They rarely compete on the rate, so if you land with no złoty, pay for the train or taxi into town by card and exchange cash in the centre.

## How to read the EUR rate

Every board shows two numbers next to the euro.

- **Buy (kupno, "we buy")**: the office buys euros from you. This is your rate when you arrive with euros.
- **Sell (sprzedaż, "we sell")**: the office sells euros to you. This applies when you change leftover złoty back.

The buy rate is always the lower one. An example with made-up numbers, not today's rates: if the board shows EUR 4.33 / 4.42, then for 200 euros you receive 200 × 4.33 = 866 złoty. The difference between the two numbers, here 0.09 PLN, is the spread. Our current rates are always on the [home page](/#rates), and the full explanation is in the guide to [buy and sell rates](/knowledge-base/buy-rate-vs-sell-rate-kantor).

## What a fair offer looks like

The spread tells you more about an office than any slogan in the window. The numbers below are examples.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Office with a narrow spread | 4.33 | 4.42 | 0.09 PLN | 2,165 PLN |
| Outlet with a wide spread | 3.95 | 4.65 | 0.70 PLN | 1,975 PLN |

That is 190 PLN lost on one exchange, and both places can truthfully display "0% commission", because commission and rate are two separate things.

A one-minute check that works anywhere in Poland:

1. Search "EUR PLN" on your phone to see the mid-market rate. Poland's central bank, NBP, also publishes an official average rate every business day around midday.
2. A fair buy rate is a few groszy below that number (one złoty is 100 groszy). Tens of groszy below means keep walking.
3. Make sure the board shows **both** rates for EUR. One attractive number on its own is usually the sell rate, which is not the one you will get.
4. Ask: "I have 200 euros. How many złoty will I get?" Hand over your notes only after you hear the answer.

## Which euro notes and coins can be exchanged

Euro cash is easy to exchange, with a few details worth knowing before you travel.

- **Current "Europa" series notes** (5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 euros) are the standard.
- **Older first-series notes** are still fine. The European Central Bank states that they always retain their value.
- **The 500 euro note** is no longer produced, but according to the ECB it remains legal tender.
- **Damaged notes** (torn, taped, stained or written on) can be a problem at any office. Inside the euro area they are replaced by national central banks, as a rule when more than half of the note remains. We buy damaged banknotes only after you contact us first; details are in our guide to [damaged and withdrawn banknotes](/exchange-damaged-withdrawn-banknotes-krakow).
- **Euro coins** are refused by most exchange offices. We buy them after you contact us first; see [exchanging euro coins in Kraków](/exchange-euro-coins-krakow). Coins in other currencies are not exchanged.

A practical tip: bring notes of 50 euros and below if you plan to exchange a little at a time. You can change one note on the first day and keep the rest as a reserve.

## Changing złoty back to euros

Leftover złoty can be changed back before you leave. This time the **sell** rate applies, the higher of the two numbers. Because you pay the spread once in each direction, the cheapest approach is not to exchange more than you expect to spend. Polish coins cannot be exchanged, so spend them on a last coffee or keep them for the next trip.

## Things to avoid

1. **Airport and hotel desks** for anything more than pocket money.
2. **ATMs that offer to charge your card in euros.** Decline the conversion and take the cash in PLN.
3. **A window with one attractive number**, or a board that shows no rates at all. Ask for the amount in złoty first. More examples are in our guide on [avoiding exchange scams in Kraków](/avoid-currency-exchange-scams-krakow).
4. **Paying in euro cash in shops and restaurants.** The rate is whatever the business decides.
5. **Exchanging everything on day one.** Change what you need and keep the rest in euros.
