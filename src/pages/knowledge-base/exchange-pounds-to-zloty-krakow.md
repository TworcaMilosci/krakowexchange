**In short:** Poland's currency is the złoty (PLN), and you will need it for anything you pay for in cash. If you are bringing sterling, exchange it in Kraków rather than at home or at the airport. Look at the **buy** rate next to GBP, compare it with the mid-market rate on your phone, and ask how many złoty you will receive before you hand over your notes. Exchange Kantor Black Sheep at Floriańska 37 buys and sells pounds with 0% commission.

Kraków is one of the most popular city breaks from the UK, and pounds are one of the three currencies we exchange every day, alongside euros and US dollars. This guide collects the questions British visitors ask most often at the counter.

## Cash or card: how much sterling to bring

You can pay by card or phone almost everywhere in Kraków: restaurants, shops, ticket machines, taxis and most museums. For those payments the only rule is to choose PLN when the terminal asks which currency to charge. The reason is explained in our guide to [paying in PLN and dynamic currency conversion](/knowledge-base/pay-in-pln-or-home-currency-dcc-krakow).

Cash is still useful for market stalls, some craft sellers, tips, public toilets and the occasional bar with a "cash only" sign. Many visitors bring a modest amount of sterling in notes, exchange part of it on the first day and keep the rest as a reserve. That avoids both ATM fees and a wallet full of złoty on the flight home.

## Why it pays to wait until you are in Kraków

Exchange offices earn their money on the gap between the rate at which they buy a currency and the rate at which they sell it. That gap, the spread, is narrow for currencies an office trades all day and wide for currencies it rarely sees.

In the UK the złoty is a minor currency, so the spread at a high-street bureau or an airport desk tends to be wide. In Kraków it is the other way round: pounds arrive every day, offices compete for them, and you can compare several boards within a few minutes' walk. Airports are the exception in both countries. They rarely compete on the rate, so if you need złoty for the first hour, the airport train ticket can be bought by card.

## How to read the GBP rate

Every board shows two numbers next to the pound.

- **Buy (kupno, "we buy")**: the office buys pounds from you. This is your rate when you arrive with sterling.
- **Sell (sprzedaż, "we sell")**: the office sells pounds to you. This applies when you want sterling back.

The buy rate is always the lower one. An example with made-up numbers: if the board shows GBP 5.19 / 5.28, then for £200 you receive 200 × 5.19 = 1,038 złoty. The difference between the two numbers, here 0.09 PLN, is the spread, and it tells you more about an office than any slogan in the window. Our current rates are always on the [home page](/#rates), and the full explanation is in the guide to [buy and sell rates](/knowledge-base/buy-rate-vs-sell-rate-kantor).

A one-minute check that works anywhere:

1. Search "GBP PLN" on your phone to see the mid-market rate.
2. A fair buy rate is a little below that number. A few groszy below is good. Tens of groszy below means keep walking.
3. Ask: "I have £200. How many złoty will I get?" Hand over your notes only after you hear the answer.

## Which pound notes can be exchanged

Not every pound note is equally easy to exchange abroad, so it is worth checking your wallet before you travel.

- **Current Bank of England polymer notes** (£5, £10, £20, £50), with either King Charles III or Queen Elizabeth II, are the standard and are accepted without questions.
- **Old paper Bank of England notes** are no longer legal tender. The paper £20 and £50 were withdrawn at the end of September 2022. Many exchange offices refuse them. We buy withdrawn notes after you contact us first, at a rate agreed individually. Details are in our guide to [damaged and withdrawn banknotes](/exchange-damaged-withdrawn-banknotes-krakow).
- **Scottish and Northern Irish notes** are issued by commercial banks and are hard to exchange outside the UK. If that is what you have, the simplest solution is to swap them for Bank of England notes before you fly. Otherwise ask us before you come.
- **Coins** are generally not exchanged abroad. Bring notes.
- **Torn, taped or written-on notes** can be a problem at any office. If a note is damaged, contact us first.

## Changing złoty back to pounds

Leftover złoty can be changed back before you leave. This time the **sell** rate applies, the higher of the two numbers. Because you pay the spread once in each direction, the cheapest approach is not to exchange more than you expect to spend. Small leftovers are often better spent on the last coffee or kept for the next trip, since coins cannot be exchanged.

## Three things to avoid

1. **Airport desks**, in the UK and in Poland, for anything more than pocket money.
2. **ATMs that offer to charge your card in pounds.** Decline the conversion and take the cash in PLN.
3. **A window with one attractive number.** If only one rate is displayed, or the board shows other currencies next to a £ symbol, ask for the amount in złoty first. More examples are in our guide on [avoiding exchange scams in Kraków](/avoid-currency-exchange-scams-krakow).
