Most visitors exchange a hundred or two at a time, and for them a few groszy on the rate hardly matter. Some people need more: a deposit on a flat, a semester of living costs, a medical or dental treatment, a car, a family event, or simply a long stay paid in cash. With several thousand euros, dollars or pounds on the table, the rate becomes real money. This guide explains how larger exchanges work in Poland and how to do one calmly.

## Why every grosz matters on a large amount

An exchange office (in Polish: *kantor*) earns its living on the spread, the gap between the rate at which it buys a currency and the rate at which it sells it. One złoty is 100 groszy, and rates are quoted to the grosz or finer. A simple rule of thumb: **one grosz on the rate is 10 PLN on every 1,000 euros, dollars or pounds**.

That is why Polish exchange offices usually have two tiers. The retail rate is for everyday amounts. The wholesale rate is for larger amounts and has a narrower spread. If you are not sure which of the two numbers on a board applies to you, start with our guide to [buy and sell rates](/knowledge-base/buy-rate-vs-sell-rate-kantor).

## Retail and wholesale rates in numbers

The figures below are made up to show the mechanism. They are not today's rates. Our current retail and wholesale tables are always on the [home page](/#rates).

Say the retail rate for the euro is 4.33 / 4.42 and the wholesale rate is 4.365 / 4.385.

| You sell to the office | At the retail rate 4.33 | At the wholesale rate 4.365 | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 euros | 4,330 PLN | 4,365 PLN | 35 PLN |
| 5,000 euros | 21,650 PLN | 21,825 PLN | 175 PLN |
| 10,000 euros | 43,300 PLN | 43,650 PLN | 350 PLN |

It works the same way in the other direction. Buying 5,000 euros at the example retail rate of 4.42 costs 22,100 PLN, and at the wholesale rate of 4.385 it costs 21,925 PLN. Again a difference of 175 PLN, this time on what you pay.

At our office the retail spread is at most 0.09 PLN and the wholesale spread is 0.01–0.03 PLN during Polish FX market hours. The wholesale rates on the website are shown for amounts of about 1,000 euros in value.

## When the wholesale rate is best

A wholesale rate follows the live market rate, and the Polish FX market works Monday to Friday from 9:00 to 17:00. During those hours we can offer the narrowest spread, 0.01–0.03 PLN.

In the evening and on Saturday the market is closed, and no office knows where it will open on the next working day. The wholesale spread is therefore slightly wider at those times. It is still better than retail, but if you can choose, come on a weekday before 17:00.

We are open Monday to Friday 09:30–20:30 and Saturday 10:00–20:30, and closed on Sunday. If your schedule only allows an evening or a Saturday, see our guide to [exchanging money late and on Saturday](/knowledge-base/currency-exchange-open-late-saturday-krakow).

## How to reserve a rate, step by step

The wholesale rates on our website are indicative. Rates move during the day, and the final offer depends on the amount and the time. So before you walk over with a large sum, confirm and reserve the rate.

1. **Check the website.** The wholesale table on the [home page](/#rates) shows you roughly what to expect.
2. **Contact us.** Call or message us on WhatsApp at +48 889 505 057. Staff speak English and Polish. You can also simply ask at the counter.
3. **Give three details:** the currency, the direction (you are selling it to us or buying it from us) and the amount. For a larger sum, add when you would like to come.
4. **Ask for the rate and the final amount.** For example: "I have 6,000 euros. Exactly how many złoty will I receive?" One number in złoty tells you everything.
5. **Agree the details of the reservation.** Ask until what time the confirmed rate is held for you, and come at the agreed time.
6. **Count your cash at the counter.** Take your time before you step away. That is normal and nobody will hurry you.

If you want to buy a larger amount of euros, dollars or pounds from us, tell us in advance. That way we can prepare the full amount and the denominations you prefer.

## ID and paperwork

For everyday amounts a Polish exchange office does not ask for any document. Under anti-money-laundering rules, identification is required only for transactions from the equivalent of **15,000 euros**. From that threshold we will ask for your passport or national ID card.

Two practical points for visitors:

- **Keep the exchange receipt.** Under the Polish Finance Ministry's regulation on exchange offices, the proof of purchase or sale shows, among other things, the currency and amount, the rate, the value in złoty and the date. It is useful if your bank at home, a landlord or a notary later asks where the cash came from.
- **Know the border rule.** When you enter or leave the European Union, cash worth 10,000 euros or more has to be declared to customs. This is what the EU's Your Europe portal states (checked September 2026). There are no uniform EU rules for travel between EU countries, so check the national rules of the country you are travelling to or from. This matters, for example, if you fly in from the United Kingdom or the United States with a large sum.

## Which notes to bring

A little preparation saves time at the counter.

- **Count and sort your notes by denomination** and write down the total. At the counter you can compare it with our count straight away.
- **Set aside damaged and old notes.** Torn, taped, washed or withdrawn banknotes are bought only after you contact us first. See our page on [damaged and withdrawn banknotes](/exchange-damaged-withdrawn-banknotes-krakow).
- **Pounds: Bank of England notes only.** We do not exchange Scottish, Northern Irish, Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man or Gibraltar notes. The details are in our guide to [exchanging pounds](/knowledge-base/exchange-pounds-to-zloty-krakow).
- **Leave coins out.** Euro coins are bought only after contacting us first, and other coins are not exchanged.

We buy and sell euros, US dollars and British pounds. We buy, but do not sell, Swiss francs, Canadian and Australian dollars, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish and Czech crowns, Hungarian forints, Japanese yen and UAE dirhams. For a larger amount in one of these currencies it is also best to ask first. Separate guides cover [euros](/knowledge-base/exchange-euros-to-zloty-krakow) and [US dollars](/knowledge-base/exchange-dollars-to-zloty-krakow).

## Staying safe with a lot of cash

Kraków's Old Town is busy and well lit, and the habits below are the same ones that apply in any city.

1. **Do not discuss the amount in the street.** Agree the rate beforehand by phone or WhatsApp, so a short confirmation at the counter is enough.
2. **Do not count money outside.** Count at the counter.
3. **Split the cash.** Two inside pockets are better than one thick envelope in your hand.
4. **Come in daylight and plan your route.** On a weekday before 17:00 you also get the best wholesale rate.
5. **Never exchange with someone who approaches you in the street.** An offer of a better rate from a stranger is not a bargain. Typical situations are described in our guide on [avoiding exchange scams in Kraków](/avoid-currency-exchange-scams-krakow).
6. **Amount first, notes second.** Ask how much you will receive and hand over your money only after you hear the answer. The rule is the same for one hundred euros and for ten thousand.
