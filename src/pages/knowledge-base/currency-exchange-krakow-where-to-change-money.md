## What really decides how much you get

Every exchange office quotes two rates: the buy rate, at which it buys foreign currency from you, and the sell rate, at which it sells it to you. The gap between them is the spread, and that is the true price of the exchange.

| | Buys EUR at | Sells EUR at | Spread | For 500 euros you get |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Office with a narrow spread | 4.33 | 4.42 | 0.09 PLN | 2,165 PLN |
| Outlet with a wide spread | 3.95 | 4.65 | 0.70 PLN | 1,975 PLN |

These are example numbers, but the difference is real: 190 PLN on a single exchange. Both places can display "0% commission" and both are telling the truth, because commission and rate are two separate things. The full explanation is in our guide to [buy and sell rates](/knowledge-base/buy-rate-vs-sell-rate-kantor).

## Is the Old Town always expensive?

That is the most common advice online, and it is only half true. Along the main tourist routes there are outlets that rely on customers being rushed and inattentive: they show one attractive number, show no rates at all, or display rates for other currencies next to familiar symbols. ## How to compare offices in one minute

1. **Check the mid-market rate.** Search "EUR PLN", "GBP PLN" or "USD PLN" on your phone.
2. **Look at both rates on the board.** A fair buy rate is a few groszy below the mid-market rate, and the sell rate a few groszy above.
3. **Work out the spread.** Subtract buy from sell. A few groszy is a good offer; tens of groszy means keep walking.
4. **Ask for the amount in your hand.** "I have 500 euros. How many złoty will I get?" Hand over your notes only after you hear the answer.

Online comparison sites for exchange offices can help too. Keep in mind that they list only the offices that have signed up, and a quoted rate may apply to larger amounts or require a reservation. Always confirm it at the counter.
