**In short:** Kraków's Christmas market on the Main Square (Rynek Główny) is planned to open on **Friday 27 November 2026** and to run until **6 January 2027**. Entry is free. Stalls take **złoty only**; last year many accepted cards, but cash is still the practical choice for mulled wine, grilled cheese and small gifts. Allow roughly **100–200 PLN per person** in cash for an evening of eating and browsing. Our exchange office at Floriańska 37 is under two minutes from the stalls.

We work two minutes from the Square and walk through the market every December on the way to work. This guide covers the practical side: when it runs, what things cost, how to pay and how to avoid carrying more cash than you need.

## Dates and opening hours in 2026

- **Opening:** planned for Friday 27 November 2026.
- **Closing:** 6 January 2027.
- **Hours:** not officially published for visitors when we wrote this guide (mid-November 2026). Last year stalls opened around 10:00 and traded late into the night. Hours differ from stall to stall, and some food and drink stalls were still serving at 23:00.
- **Christmas and New Year:** opening on 24–26 December and 1 January had not been announced. Expect shorter hours on Christmas Eve.

Two smaller fairs are listed for the same period, on Mały Rynek and on St. Mary Magdalene Square, a few minutes' walk from the Main Square.

The best time to come is around dusk, when the lights go on and the sky behind St. Mary's Basilica turns pink. Weekday afternoons are calmer than weekend evenings.

## What you will find there

Rows of wooden stalls fill a large part of the Square around the Cloth Hall. The list below describes last year's market; this year's details will be known on opening day.

- **Food:** grilled oscypek (smoked sheep's cheese) with cranberry, pierogi, żurek and other soups, sausages from the grill, bread with lard and pickles, gingerbread and sweets.
- **Drinks:** mulled wine (*grzaniec*), hot chocolate, tea with raspberry syrup.
- **Gifts:** hand-painted glass baubles, wooden toys, amber, ceramics, wool and sheepskin, Christmas decorations.
- **On the Square:** the city Christmas tree, a carousel for children and a stage with carols and folk groups.

One tradition is unique to Kraków: the nativity scene competition. On the first Thursday of December, makers bring their *szopki*, intricate models inspired by the city's churches and towers, to the Adam Mickiewicz monument on the Square. The tradition is on the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage, and the best scenes are then displayed around the city.

## What things cost

Prices at the market are higher than in the rest of the city. You are paying for the location. Prices for 2026 are set on opening day, so treat the figures below, from recent editions, as a guide.

| Item | Approximate price in recent years |
|---|---|
| Grilled oscypek with cranberry | from about 17 PLN |
| Mulled wine, small cup (200 ml) | 18–25 PLN |
| Mulled wine, large or flavoured | 35–45 PLN |
| Żurek or another soup | about 35 PLN |
| Portion of pierogi | 42–50 PLN |
| Hand-painted bauble or small gift | from a few dozen PLN |

For comparison, a full lunch in a traditional milk bar a few streets away costs 25–45 PLN. Many visitors snack at the market and eat a proper meal elsewhere. Typical prices across the city are in our guide to [the cost of a weekend in Kraków](/knowledge-base/how-much-money-weekend-in-krakow).

## Cash or card at the stalls

Stalls accept **złoty only**. You cannot pay in euros, pounds or dollars.

Last year many stalls had card terminals, but not all, and connections can be slow on a crowded evening. Cash is quicker in a queue and works everywhere, so it is worth having notes and coins in your pocket.

**How much to bring:** for an evening of mulled wine, something from the grill and a small gift or two, 100–200 PLN per person in cash is usually enough. Add more only if you plan to buy larger presents, and ask the stallholder first whether cards are accepted.

If you pay by card, choose **PLN** when the terminal asks which currency to charge. The reason is in our guide to [paying in PLN and dynamic currency conversion](/knowledge-base/pay-in-pln-or-home-currency-dcc-krakow).

## Practical tips for the market

1. **Exchange a modest amount.** Take what you plan to spend that evening. Unspent złoty have to be changed back.
2. **Keep small notes.** Stallholders appreciate 10, 20 and 50 PLN notes more than a 200.
3. **Mind your pockets.** The market is safe, but any dense crowd attracts pickpockets. Keep your wallet in an inside pocket.
4. **Mugs may carry a deposit.** Last year some stalls served mulled wine in a decorated mug with a refundable deposit; ask when you order.
5. **Dress for standing still.** December evenings are often below zero, and you will be outside for longer than you expect.
6. **Avoid standalone ATMs around the Square** that offer to charge you in your home currency. Use an ATM at a bank branch and decline the conversion.
